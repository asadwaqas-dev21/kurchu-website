01 — Services

Everything a serious app needs, under one roof.

Engage us for a single discipline or the whole product. Either way, design and engineering sit in the same team, in the same rituals, accountable to the same outcome.

Before a line of code, we pressure-test the idea: who it's for, what they'll pay for, and the smallest version that proves it. You leave with a scope you can defend to investors and a plan your engineers can estimate.

  • Product discovery
  • MVP definition
  • Competitive analysis
  • Feature prioritisation
  • Technical planning

Research-led journeys, then a visual system with real taste. Every key flow is prototyped and tested with users before it reaches engineering — so the expensive mistakes happen in Figma, not in production.

04 — Process

Eight stages. No surprises.

Every stage ends with something you can hold — a document, a prototype, a build. You see progress every week in a live demo, and you always know what's being decided and why.

01/ 08

Discovery

You receiveProduct briefProblem, audience, success metrics and scope.
  2. 01

    Discovery

    1–2 weeks

    Workshops with your team, interviews with real users, a hard look at competitors. We find the riskiest assumption and decide how to test it.

    Decisions made

    • Who the first users are
    • The problem worth paying for

    Your involvement

    • Two workshops, 2–3 hours each
    • Access to customers if available
    You receiveProduct brief
  3. 02

    Strategy

    1 week

    We turn findings into a prioritised roadmap, draw the MVP line and recommend a technical approach — native or cross-platform — with costs for each option.

    Decisions made

    • MVP scope and success metrics
    • Platform and stack choice

    Your involvement

    • Roadmap review session
    • Sign-off on scope
    You receiveFeature roadmap
  4. 03

    UX

    2–3 weeks

    Information architecture, user journeys and wireframes for every key flow. We test with users before visual design, while change is still nearly free.

    Decisions made

    • Navigation model
    • Content and data per screen

    Your involvement

    • Weekly flow reviews
    • Observe user sessions
    You receiveValidated user flow
  5. 04

    UI Design

    2–4 weeks

    A visual system with real character — type, colour, motion and components — then every screen in high fidelity, including the empty, loading and error states.

    Decisions made

    Your involvement

    • Final design sign-off

  6. 05

    Engineering

    6–16 weeks

    Two-week sprints with a demo at the end of each. You install real builds on your own phone every week — not slides about progress.

    Decisions made

    • Trade-offs as they surface

    Your involvement

    • Fortnightly demo, 45 minutes
    • Shared Slack channel
    You receiveProduction builds
  7. 06

    Testing

    Continuous + 1–2 weeks

    Automated tests run on every merge. Before release, a full regression across our device matrix, performance profiling and a security review.

    Decisions made

    • User acceptance testing
    • Release sign-off
    You receiveQA report

    Launch

    Store listings, screenshots, privacy declarations and review notes, handled. We ship as a phased rollout with crash monitoring and analytics live from minute one.

    Decisions made

    • Launch date and rollout pace

    • Launch-day stand-by

07 — Why Kestrel

A product team, not a vendor.

We're deliberately small and senior. We take on a handful of products at a time so the people you meet in the pitch are the people who build.

i.

Product thinking before code

We challenge assumptions before expensive engineering begins — and we'll say so if a feature shouldn't be built.

ii.

One team, start to finish

Designers and engineers work in the same sprints. Nothing gets thrown over a wall between departments.

iii.

Senior hands on critical decisions

Architecture, data models and security are owned by engineers with a decade or more of shipping behind them.

iv.

Transparent delivery

Fixed scopes per phase, visible milestones, fortnightly demos and a shared channel. You never chase us for status.

v.

Built for the next version

Architecture accounts for the features on your roadmap, so version two is an extension, not a rewrite.

vi.

Business context matters

Every technical choice is weighed against your runway, your market and your revenue model — not ours.
